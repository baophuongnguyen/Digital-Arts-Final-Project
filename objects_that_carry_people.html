Today, many of us carry our memories in smartphones, laptops, and cloud storage. Photos, messages, and digital archives help us remember the people, places, and moments that shape our lives.

But what happened before these technologies existed? For soldiers during the Resistance War against America, memories were often carried in a different way.

This project invites you into Mr. Nguyễn Đình Thảo's home through an interactive environment. These artifacts belonged to one veteran, but they reflect the experiences of countless soldiers.

Exhibition Guide: How to Immerse Yourself

Navigating the Space

  • Change views: There are 3 views of the room. Switch between them by clicking the menu (top-left corner) or the arrows on the floor.
  • Look around: Click and drag the screen to view the entire room.
  • Autorotate: To pause the automatic rotation of the space, click the button in the top-right corner.

Discovering the Objects

  • Find the artifacts: Click on the i icons for a closer look and to uncover the stories behind them.
  • Explore media: Play with the 3D views or scroll to view all photos if an artifact features a carousel.
  • Listen to history: As you read, click on the yellow underlined sentences to listen to Mr. Thảo’s original recorded voice. Click again to close the audio.
  • Track your progress: You can see the list of objects in a room view on the top middle bar. Make sure to find all of them!

Public Memory

The Veteran in the Press

History is often written in broad strokes, but it is anchored by physical things. While Mr. Thảo’s private diaries reveal the quiet, internal struggles of a soldier, these published clippings show how his story and his artifacts stepped out of the jungle and into the collective memory of the nation.

Here, the press captures the exact moments his belongings transitioned from tools of survival into symbols of peace. Through these public records, we see a collection of war-torn gear validated as heritage, and we witness the ultimate resilience of the man who carried them: returning from the battlefield to hold a piece of chalk once again.

01. The Process

This project began with a simple question: How can we preserve and communicate personal histories through digital media?

After deciding to focus on the theme of human experiences during wartime, we reached out to Mr. Nguyễn Đình Thảo, the grandfather of our team member Hoàng Nhi. As a former soldier who fought during the Vietnam War and still preserves many of his personal belongings from that period, Mr. Thảo became the central storyteller of our project.

Main Steps of Our Project:

  • ✦ Conducting an in-depth oral history interview (project foundation) on stories beyond the functions of each artifact: the memories, experiences, and emotions attached to the objects.
  • ✦ Capturing a 360-degree photographic environment (Mr. Thảo’s current home where he stored the artifacts) and artifacts using Scaniverse (Mesh) and RealityScan.
  • ✦ Digitizing historical documents, including certificates, commendations, newspaper articles, diaries, and poetry notebooks using AI and VietOCR.
  • ✦ Designing the narrative structure of the exhibition.
  • ✦ Creating a simple interactive 360-degree virtual environment with hotspots using Marzipano.
  • ✦ Incorporating Mr. Thảo’s original recorded voice throughout the exhibition.
  • ✦ Deploying and leveraging our website and virtual tour using Github and AI-assisted tools such as ChatGPT and Gemini.

Through this process, we transformed personal memories, physical artifacts, and historical documents into an accessible digital archive that preserves and shares one individual's experience while connecting it to broader questions of memory, history, and human resilience.

03. Final Reflection

Documenting the objects helps us understand the person behind them and the situation in which they were the person’s fortune.

Each artifact revealed a different aspect of life during wartime. A hammock was not simply a place to sleep, but a shelter against months of rain in the Trường Sơn forests. A helmet was not only protective equipment, but also a seat, a water container, and a silent witness to a moment when a fragment of shrapnel nearly took a life. A notebook contained more than written words; it preserved poems, memories, and thoughts of a young teacher who was separated from the woman he loved and did not know whether he would ever return home.

One of the most meaningful aspects of this project was the personal connection many team members felt with Mr. Thảo's experiences. Several of us have grandparents who also lived through the war, yet we had rarely asked them about their lives during that period. Listening to Mr. Thảo made us realize how much history exists within our own families, often preserved only through memories, stories, and everyday objects. It reminded us that the generation who experienced these events firsthand will not be here forever to tell their stories, making preservation more important than ever.

As students in a Digital Humanities course, we also learned that technology knowledge and skills are valuable as they enhance our ability to preserve, communicate, and make human stories accessible to others. With a view to creating the most immersive and realistic experience online, we have learned how to use and integrate many tools and techniques throughout the projects, and they all paid off with everything we have been able to keep forever.

Most importantly, this project (and other Mini’s one!) changed the way we think about history. We no longer see history solely as something contained in textbooks or official records. We now understand that history can also live in a worn hammock, a faded backpack, a handwritten letter, or a notebook filled with poems. These ordinary objects carry extraordinary stories because they carry traces of the people who once relied on them.

This project taught us that preserving history is preserving humanity, helping to shape how we understand ourselves today. We entered this project hoping to document a collection of wartime objects. We leave it having gained a deeper appreciation for the lives and memories of those who have sacrificed many things for our nation.
